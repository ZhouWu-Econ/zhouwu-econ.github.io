Testing One-Way versus Two-Way Clustering: Non-Normal Limits and Bootstrap Inference

with Yimeng Xie, Tao Zeng, and Qiankun Zhou

Under review at Journal of Econometrics.

Accepted for presentation at the 3rd Greater Bay Econometrics Study Group (GBESG 2026).

Related fields: Cluster-robust inference; cross-sectional and panel data models.

October 2026 · Paper [PDF]

We develop a wild cluster bootstrap test for choosing between one-way and two-way clustering when standard normal critical values may be invalid.

Abstract

Influential work by MacKinnon et al. (2023b, Journal of Econometrics) proposes the score-variance (SV) test for choosing between two nested levels of one-way clustering and establishes asymptotic normality of the test statistic. We apply the SV statistic to a distinct problem of testing one-way against two-way clustering, and show that, under the null, its limit distribution is a weighted combination of chi-square variables and an independent normal component, where the weights are determined by the spectral structure of the cluster-score covariance matrix. Consequently, standard normal critical values need not be valid, while the appropriate nonstandard critical values depend on an unknown covariance structure.

We therefore propose the score-variance ratio (SV-R), a variant of the SV statistic, together with the wild cluster bootstrap. We establish the validity of the bootstrap SV-R test, which has asymptotically correct rejection frequency without requiring knowledge of the specific form of the limit distribution. Monte Carlo experiments show that the bootstrap SV-R procedure controls size and has useful power under different data generating processes. An empirical application illustrates its implementation.

Joint Selection of Bayesian Model and Predictive Distribution under Misspecification

with Yong Li, Jun Yu, and Tao Zeng

Under review at Journal of Econometrics. [PDF]

Accepted for presentation at the 2024 Asian Meeting of the Econometric Society (AMES 2024).

Related fields: Bayesian econometrics; time-series models; model selection.

We introduce a sandwich Bayesian predictive distribution and an information criterion for jointly selecting models and predictive distributions under misspecification.

Abstract

Many model selection criteria, including the Deviance Information Criterion (DIC, Spiegelhalter et al., 2002), evaluate predictive performance of a model via the plug-in predictive distribution. We propose the simultaneous choice of a Bayesian model and a predictive distribution under model misspecification. We introduce a sandwich Bayesian predictive distribution that averages the likelihood of future data over the sandwich posterior of Müller (2013), and derive the asymptotic expansion of its frequentist risk.

It is shown that the sandwich Bayesian predictive distribution always leads to lower asymptotic risk than the standard Bayesian predictive distribution and, under a trace condition, has lower asymptotic risk than the plug-in predictive distribution. Based on the expansion, we propose the Sandwich Predictive Information Criterion (SPIC). SPIC, when used in conjunction with DICM (Li et al., 2020), provides a framework for joint model and predictive distribution selection. Finally, simulation and empirical studies examine how well SPIC performs in finite samples and assess the effectiveness of our proposed joint selection procedure.

Robust Matrix Completion via Quantile-Based Intervals: Inference and Applications to Treatment Effects

with Siwei Jiang, Bin Peng, and Qiankun Zhou

Accepted for presentation at the 26th China Economics Annual Conference.

Related fields: Causal inference; panel data models.

We develop a matrix completion method based on quantile factor models that directly yields confidence intervals for missing entries. Applications to causal inference and policy evaluation with panel data enable statistical inference on time-varying individual treatment effects.

Related fields: Panel data models; cluster-robust inference.

We propose a test of the clustering level used in cluster-robust inference for panel data models. The method accommodates fixed-effects, instrumental-variable, and dynamic panel specifications.

Modeling Dependence Structure of Multiple Outcomes in Causal Inference: A Bayesian Approach

with Yaohan Chen, Tao Zeng, and Qiankun Zhou

Related fields: Causal inference; Bayesian econometrics; panel data models.

We develop a semiparametric copula distribution regression framework for multiple outcomes. Using Bayesian methods, we study how policy shocks affect dependence across outcomes, extending the focus of causal inference beyond treatment effects on a single outcome.
